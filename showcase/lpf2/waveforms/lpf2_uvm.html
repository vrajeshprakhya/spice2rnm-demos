What the run does: it steps the model's input through 41 levels from 0.00 to 1.80 V, waits at each until the output stops moving (steps of 125 ns until two in a row change it by less than the settling limit) and reads the output once. Then it plays a small sine, 3.6 mV on top of 0.90 V, at 10 frequencies from 199.5 kHz to 50.12 MHz: 6 cycles to settle, then gain and phase measured over the next 8. A randomised tail repeats points of either kind. The input is built from steps, 64 per cycle, so the sines going in are staircases: that is what the model actually receives.

Every reading is compared with what ngspice measured on the circuit: the DC levels from a DC sweep, the gain and phase from an AC analysis. Blue is ngspice, red is the model. At a DC check the blue ring is ngspice's value and the red dot is the model's reading. In an AC window (shaded, labelled with its frequency) the blue band is the sine ngspice's gain and phase predict for this drive, centred on the model's own average level there (the level is the DC checks' business); the red line is what the model did.

The lower panel puts every check on one scale: its error divided by its tolerance, so a point above the dashed line at 1 would be a failure. Dots are DC errors and AC gain errors, rings are AC phase errors. Hover over any point to read the check in full.

The scoreboard's own summary for this run: dc checks=49 failed=0 | worst err 0.000000 V at in = 0.045000 V (tol 0.003600 V); ac checks=15 failed=0 | worst 0.0927 dB at 5.01187e+07 Hz (tol 0.2500 dB); worst 0.39 deg at 199526 Hz (tol 4.00 deg).

How the phase checks read. The bridge holds each drive value for one step (1/64 of a cycle) and reads the output at the MIDDLE of the step, where the held value stands for the sine, so the reading and the drive phase it is scored against describe the same instant (spice2rnm #74: reading at the step's end used to add a lead of 360°/128 = 2.8° to every check).

How this page was made: the generated environment was re-run unchanged except for three lines added to a copy of top_rc_lpf2_ms.sv that record its stimulus and response nets. Every check above is that run's own log line (phases included). This page also re-measures each AC window from the recording the way the bridge does; its gains agree with the scoreboard's to within 2.1e-04 dB, which is the check that the recording is what the scoreboard saw.

Every point the run recorded is in this page (times to 1 fs, voltages to 0.1 µV), and it loads nothing from the network. Built by harness/uvm_wave.py in the spice2rnm demos.
